## Candidates

Numbers below are expectations to check; the sim measures the claim.

1. Orbit passing lane. Same 400 km circular orbit, two ships side by
   side, one fires 10 m/s forward. Expect it about 170 km behind after
   one 92-minute lap and 35 km higher at the far side (da = 2 a dv / v =
   17.6 km, dT = 21.7 s, 7.67 km/s x 21.7 s = 166 km). Pillar: physics
   (orbital). Two-body RK4 or exact Kepler, one orbit loops. Hook: "Fire
   your engine forward. Do you pull ahead?" Peg: JUICE 2026-09-30,
   Sputnik 1957-10-04. Sources: howthingsfly.si.edu, arXiv 1908.02592.
   Accepted: strongest two-panel same-input surprise, under one hour.
2. Galilean cannon. Tennis ball on a basketball from 1 m beside a tennis
   ball alone. Expect about 7 m (ratio 10.7 gives (3M - m)/(M + m) =
   2.66x speed, 7.1x height; 9x in the limit). Pillar: physics.
   Event-driven elastic 1D collisions. Hook: "Drop them together. How high
   does the small ball go?" Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilean_cannon,
   iop.org episode 13. Accepted: viral demo, one number, under one hour.
3. Gravity train. Capsule through the centre of a uniform Earth beside
   one in a 340 km chord. Expect 42.2 min for both (pi sqrt(R/g)), top
   speeds 7.9 km/s and about 210 m/s, period 84.4 min so the time-lapse
   loops. Pillar: physics. Exact SHM or RK4 along each tunnel. Hook:
   "Fall through the Earth. How long to the other side?" Source: AJP 88
   499, damninteresting.com/the-gravity-express. Accepted: same-time
   payoff on very different tunnels; state the 38 min real-Earth caveat
   in the description.
4. Slinky drop. 100 coils stretched under gravity beside a rigid rod,
   both released. Expect the bottom coil still for about 0.32 s on a 1.5
   m stretch (centre of mass at L/3: sqrt(2L/3g)) while the top falls the
   whole 1.5 m, 3 g on average; the rod bottom moves at once. Pillar:
   physics (waves). Mass-spring chain with coils that pile up
   inelastically, semi-implicit Euler at small step. Hook: "Drop a
   stretched slinky. Does the bottom fall?" Source: jdh.hamkins.org,
   bu.edu/moss JSV paper. Accepted: viral debate, continuous motion,
   about 1.5 hours for the contact handling.
5. Swing pumping. 5 degree start, no push; stand at the bottom and squat
   at the top (10 percent length change) versus the reversed rhythm.
   Expect the right rhythm past 60 degrees in about ten swings (height
   grows (L/L')^3 per half swing) and the reversed one shrinking; at rest
   nothing starts. Pillar: physics (resonance). Variable-length pendulum
   ODE, RK4. Hook: "How does a swing go higher with nobody pushing?"
   Source: AJP 64 215, Glendinning EJP preprint. Accepted: the Kapitza
   family (822 views), about one hour.
6. Quantum tunnelling. Wave packet with 80 percent of the wall energy
   beside a classical ball. Expect a few percent through (tune the wall
   for about 3 percent), 0 for the ball, and halving the width to
   multiply the share by about ten. Pillar: physics (waves), the first
   quantum subject; the claim is a share, so state it as such. Split-step
   Fourier on 4,096 points, no seed. Hook: "Can a ball go through a
   wall?" Peg: Schrodinger centenary, Nobel physics 2026-10-06 (2025
   prize was macroscopic tunnelling). Source: schroedinger100.univie.ac.at,
   fluxnote list. Accepted for Nobel week; ranked below the mechanical
   ideas because a colour blob risks reading as a drawing.
7. Gravity assist. Same probe, 5 km/s relative to an Earth-mass planet on
   a 30 km/s orbit, passing behind versus in front at the same distance.
   Expect about +5 and -5 km/s relative to the Sun (deflection 67 deg at
   one Earth radius altitude gives 2 v_inf sin(delta/2) = 5.6 km/s).
   Pillar: physics (orbital). Sun-frame RK4 with a moving planet, small
   step near the planet. Hook: "Can a planet throw a spaceship faster for
   free?" Peg: JUICE 2026-09-30 (2024 flyby measured -4.8 km/s). Source:
   esa.int JUICE page. Accepted; about two hours, so ranked after the
   passing lane which uses the same peg for less work.
8. Metronome sync. Two metronomes a quarter beat apart on a rolling board
   versus a fixed table. Expect a lock within about a minute on the board
   and no lock on the table. Pillar: physics (oscillators). Pantaleone
   2002 model: escapement (van der Pol) pendulums coupled through the
   base, RK4. Hook: "Why do these two end up ticking together?" Source:
   Pantaleone AJP 70 992 (PDF mirror above), PMC12469052. Accepted;
   viral subject; about two hours, the escapement term needs tuning.
9. Rogue wave. 200 random-phase sine waves, spectrum peaked at 10 s,
   10,000 waves past a buoy. Expect about 3 crests above twice the
   significant height (Rayleigh 1 in 2,981), tallest near 2.2x. Pillar:
   waves (new corner of physics). Numpy sum, zero-crossing counter, seed
   on screen. Hook: "Every wave is 1 m. Can they make a 3 m wave?"
   Source: physicstoday.aip.org quick study. Accepted with a counter
   caveat: the payoff is a count, so keep the moving surface on screen.
10. Rainbow angle. 10,000 parallel rays into a drop, one bounce, red and
    violet. Expect the pile at 42.4 deg red, 40.6 violet, nothing past
    it, then the two-bounce pile near 51 deg. Pillar: optics (new corner
    of physics). Exact Snell, PIL lines, no seed. Hook: "Why is every
    rainbow the same size?" Source: st-andrews.ac.uk/~bds2/optics/rainbow,
    arXiv 2508.05644. Accepted; a sweep rather than motion, so ranked low.
11. Mach cone. Rings every 0.1 s from a source at Mach 0.5, 1 and 2.
    Expect no cone, a flat wall, and exactly 30.0 deg. Pillar: waves.
    Exact circles, loops. Hook: "What does sound look like when you
    outrun it?" Source: fluxnote list (whip sonic boom). Accepted as the
    cheap replacement for the failed Kelvin wake; modest surprise.
12. Dropped versus fired bullet. Same 1.5 m, one dropped, one fired level
    at 360 m/s, in a vacuum and in air with quadratic drag. Expect 0.553 s
    for both in vacuum and the fired one a few hundredths later in air
    (drag on the fast bullet has an upward part). Pillar: physics (viral
    debate). 2D drag ODE, RK4. Hook: "Drop one bullet, fire one. Which
    lands first?" Source: the MythBusters result (39 ms) is folklore, so
    the sim carries the number. Accepted, ranked low: the air answer
    depends on the drag choice, so the vacuum tie is the headline.

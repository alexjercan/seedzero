Two cars, the same brakes. Twice the speed. How much farther to stop? Both hit the brakes at the line. Left car, fifty kilometers an hour. Right car, twice that: one hundred.
At the spot where the slow car has stopped, the fast car is doing about eighty seven kilometers an hour. Both cars shed speed at the same rate. The fast car has twice the speed to shed, so it takes twice as long to stop, and it is faster the whole way.
Twice the speed. How much farther to stop? Four times as far. Twelve meters, then forty nine meters.

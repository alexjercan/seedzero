Why is every rainbow the same size? Ten thousand rays of red light hit one drop of water, at every height from the middle to the top. Each ray bends going in, bounces once off the back, and bends again coming out.
Rays near the middle come straight back. Higher up, they leave wider and wider, until the angle stops at forty two degrees and turns back. Light piles up where it stops, and not one ray gets past it.
Violet light bends a little more, so its pile sits at forty degrees, just inside the red.
Why is every rainbow the same size? Because light leaving a drop piles up at forty two degrees.

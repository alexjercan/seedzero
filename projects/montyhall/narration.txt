Ten thousand rounds of the same game, all at once.
Three doors, one prize. You pick a door.
The host, who knows where the prize hides, opens a different door: always a losing one.
Then you choose: stay, or switch.
We scored every round both ways, with the same doors and the same picks.
Staying won three thousand three hundred eight rounds.
The switch won six thousand six hundred ninety two: two out of three.
Here is why. Your first pick is right one time in three.
Every time it is wrong, the host has no choice, and the switch door hides the prize.
The switch only loses when your first guess was already right.
Same doors, same luck. One choice doubled the wins.
